Real estate runs on speed. The agent who follows up first, keeps their CRM current, and never lets a transaction stall is the one who wins the listing and closes the deal. The problem is that this work — follow-up, data entry, coordination — is exactly what pulls you away from clients and showings.

A **[real estate virtual assistant](/industries/real-estate)** is a professional who takes on that recurring work so your pipeline keeps moving even when you're not the one moving it. Here's what that looks like in practice.

## What a real estate virtual assistant handles

### Lead follow-up
New leads go cold fast. A VA sends timely email and SMS follow-up, qualifies interest, and keeps prospects warm until they're ready to talk to you directly.

### CRM and pipeline management
Keeping contacts clean, deals tagged correctly, and every stage of your pipeline up to date — so the data you're making decisions on is actually trustworthy. Many of our VAs work directly in [GoHighLevel](/services/gohighlevel-virtual-assistant), which a growing number of agents and teams use to run their entire pipeline.

### Transaction coordination
Chasing signatures, tracking deadlines, and organizing the paperwork behind every closing — the detail work that derails a transaction when nobody owns it.

### Scheduling and calendar management
Booking showings, coordinating with other agents, and protecting your calendar so you're not double-booked or scrambling between appointments.

### Listings and social media
Getting new listings posted promptly, and keeping your [social media](/services/social-media-management) active between them, so your pipeline of future clients doesn't go quiet.

### Administrative support
Data entry, document organization, and the general [admin work](/services/administrative-virtual-assistant) that piles up around a busy real estate business.

## Signs it's time to hire one

You'll likely benefit from a real estate VA if:

- **Leads go cold** because follow-up isn't consistent
- Your **CRM is a mess** of half-entered or duplicate contacts
- **Transaction paperwork** eats time you'd rather spend with clients
- Listings and posts go out **late, or not at all**
- You're **turning away business** because there aren't enough hours in the day

If any of those sound familiar, the bottleneck usually isn't effort — it's that one person is doing work that should be split two ways.

## What to look for in a real estate VA

Not all virtual assistant experience transfers cleanly to real estate. Look for someone who:

- Is familiar with **real estate CRMs** (GoHighLevel, Follow Up Boss, and similar tools)
- Understands the **urgency of lead follow-up** in a way general admin support doesn't
- Can **follow a transaction checklist** without missing steps
- **Communicates clearly** with clients, agents, and vendors on your behalf

The best real estate VAs combine administrative precision with an understanding of how a deal actually moves from lead to close.

## A typical week

What this looks like in practice varies by business, but a common split is:

- **Daily:** new-lead follow-up, inbox and calendar triage, CRM updates as deals move
- **Weekly:** pipeline review, transaction deadline checks, listing and social posts scheduled
- **As needed:** showing coordination, document chasing, research on a new area or listing

None of it requires your judgment on strategy or client relationships — it just requires someone reliable doing it consistently, which is exactly what tends to slip when you're the one trying to do it between showings.
