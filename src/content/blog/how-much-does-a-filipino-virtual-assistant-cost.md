It's the first question almost every business owner asks: *how much does a Filipino virtual assistant actually cost?*

The honest answer is that it depends — but that's not a cop-out. The cost of a [virtual assistant](/services) varies for specific, understandable reasons, and once you know what drives the number, you can budget with confidence instead of guessing. Here's the full picture.

## What actually drives the cost

Five factors move the price up or down more than anything else:

1. **Experience.** A professional with several years of hands-on work commands more than someone just starting out — and usually needs far less hand-holding.
2. **Specialization.** A general administrative VA costs less than a specialist. A [GoHighLevel virtual assistant](/services/gohighlevel-virtual-assistant), a marketing-automation expert, or an experienced executive assistant sits at the higher end because the skill is harder to find.
3. **Hours and commitment.** A few hours a week is priced differently than a full-time role. More hours often means a better effective rate.
4. **Engagement type.** Ongoing support, a fixed-scope project, and full-time placement are priced in different ways (more on this below).
5. **Freelance vs. managed agency.** A freelancer you find and manage yourself may show a lower headline rate; a managed partner includes vetting, matching, and ongoing support in the price.

## The common pricing models

You'll generally encounter cost quoted in one of four ways:

- **Hourly** — you pay for time worked. Flexible, and good for variable or unpredictable workloads.
- **Part-time retainer** — a set number of hours per week or month for a fixed fee. Predictable, and the most popular option for ongoing support.
- **Full-time** — a dedicated professional working your full schedule. The best effective rate per hour, suited to a substantial, steady workload.
- **Project-based** — a fixed scope for a defined deliverable, such as a [CRM setup or migration](/services/crm-automation). You pay for the outcome, not the hours.

## General market ranges

Rather than quote a single number that would be misleading, here's a rough, general guide to how the market tends to price Filipino virtual talent. Treat these as broad context, not fixed rates — the actual figure depends on the five factors above.

| Type of support | General market range (per hour) |
|---|---|
| General administrative VA | lower end |
| Experienced executive / specialized admin | mid-range |
| Marketing, social media, content | mid-range |
| GoHighLevel, CRM, and automation specialists | higher end |

The pattern is simple: the more specialized and experienced the professional, the higher the rate — and usually the higher the return, because they solve harder problems in less time.

## Why the cheapest option is rarely the cheapest

It's tempting to sort by lowest rate and pick the bottom of the list. In practice, that's often the most expensive route:

- **Rework costs more than the savings.** A bargain hire who needs everything corrected can cost you more in your own time than a capable professional would have charged outright.
- **Turnover is expensive.** Re-hiring and re-onboarding every few months burns time and momentum.
- **Mistakes in the wrong place hurt.** A misconfigured automation or a botched CRM migration can cost real revenue.

The goal isn't the lowest rate — it's the **best value**: someone experienced enough to work independently and get it right the first time.

## Freelance vs. agency: what you're paying for

A freelancer's headline rate can look lower because you're absorbing the hidden work yourself — screening candidates, vetting skills, onboarding, and covering the risk if it doesn't work out. A [managed partnership](/why-hire-filipino-virtual-assistants) folds that work into the price: the professional is pre-vetted, matched to your needs, and supported over time. For many owners, that trade is worth it — the price includes peace of mind, not just hours.
